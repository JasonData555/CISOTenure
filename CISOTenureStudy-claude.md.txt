## WHAT THIS PROJECT IS


A publication-grade study of North American CISO tenure spanning 2017–2025, designed to withstand scrutiny from journalists at outlets like the Wall Street Journal. The study measures how long CISOs stay in their roles and how that has changed across pre-COVID, COVID, and post-COVID periods.


**Primary deliverable:** A statistically defensible median CISO tenure figure with trend analysis over 8 years.
**Secondary deliverables:** Hazard rate (when CISOs are most likely to exit), era comparison curves, and sample composition tables.


---


## WHAT THIS PROJECT IS NOT


- It is NOT a compensation study
- It is NOT a stratified analysis by company size, industry, or region (see COVARIATE RULES below)
- It is NOT a survey — data comes from scraped LinkedIn work history
- It is NOT person-level analysis — the unit of observation is the role episode


---


## DATA FOUNDATION


### Source
~1,200 LinkedIn profiles from Hitch Partners' proprietary database. Hitch specializes in passive candidate sourcing — these are not self-selected active job seekers. Profiles were selected by data completeness: must have enough fields to identify CISO-level roles and scrape work history.


### Visibility Bias (only bias to disclose)
Profiles are professionally visible CISOs — identifiable through LinkedIn presence, referrals, or public activity. Truly invisible CISOs (no LinkedIn, no public footprint) are underrepresented. This population is small, skews older and longer-tenured, and warrants one disclosure sentence. Do not overstate this limitation.


### What "completeness" means for selection
A profile qualifies if it has: a valid LinkedIn URL, at least one identifiable CISO-level title, at least one role with a start date, and industry/company size inferable from Hitch records.

## UNIT OF ANALYSIS


**The role episode — not the person.**


One CISO who held three CISO-level roles contributes three episodes. This is intentional and standard in labor economics research. It is explicitly disclosed in the methodology note.


Expected yield:
- 1,200 profiles × 2–3 episodes average = 2,400–3,600 raw episodes
- After title filtering and QA: ~1,800–2,500 valid episodes
- Completed (non-censored) episodes: ~1,200–1,850
- This is a statistically powerful dataset


---


## CENSORING — CRITICAL


Current roles are RIGHT-CENSORED observations. They are NOT excluded and NOT treated as complete.


**Every current role must be tagged:**
- `is_censored = True`
- `scrape_date` recorded per individual profile (not batch date)
- `duration_months_to_scrape_date` calculated as the observed duration


**In KM analysis:**
- `duration` = `duration_months` for completed; `duration_months_to_scrape_date` for censored
- `event_observed` = `not is_censored` (True = exited, False = still in role)


Dropping censored observations biases toward shorter tenures. Including them uncorrected biases toward longer. KM handles both correctly. This is non-negotiable.


---


## WITHIN-PERSON CORRELATION — CRITICAL


Multiple episodes from the same person are NOT statistically independent. Standard KM assumes independence. Violating this produces artificially narrow confidence intervals.


**The fix: cluster standard errors by profile_id in all KM and Cox models.**


In lifelines, use:
```python
from lifelines import CoxPHFitter


cph = CoxPHFitter()
cph.fit(df, duration_col='duration_months', event_col='event_observed',
       cluster_col='profile_id')
```


For KM curves with clustered SE, use the `KaplanMeierFitter` with bootstrap confidence intervals grouped by `profile_id`, or report the Cox-derived median as the primary figure and KM as visual only.


**Every analysis function must cluster on profile_id. No exceptions.**


Add this check to `analysis_guards.py`:
```python
def assert_clustering_enabled(kwargs: dict):
   if 'cluster_col' not in kwargs or kwargs['cluster_col'] != 'profile_id':
       raise ValueError(
           "All survival models must cluster standard errors by profile_id "
           "to account for within-person correlation across episodes. "
           "Add cluster_col='profile_id' to your fitter call."
       )
```

## COVARIATE RULES — HARD CONSTRAINTS


Company size, industry, and region were captured at scrape time (2025), not at episode start. These attributes change materially over 8 years. They CANNOT be used as KM stratification variables.


| Variable | Use | Forbidden Use |
|---|---|---|
| `company_size_tier` | Sample composition table only | KM stratification |
| `industry_sector` / `industry_normalized` | Sample composition table only | KM stratification |
| `profile_region` | Sample composition table only | KM stratification |
| `episode_start_era` | PRIMARY analytical variable — KM stratification ✓ | N/A |
| `start_year` | Cohort trend chart ✓ | N/A |
| `profile_id` | Clustering identifier ✓ | N/A |


The `analysis_guards.py` file enforces this at runtime. Import and call guard functions at the top of every analysis block.


**One-sentence disclosure for methodology note:**
*"Company size, industry, and geographic region are reported for sample composition purposes only; because these attributes can change materially over an 8-year study window through acquisitions, growth, and relocation, they are not used as stratification variables in survival analysis."*


**One-sentence disclosure for region specifically:**
*"LinkedIn does not provide historical location data for past role episodes, making episode-level geographic assignment unreliable across an 8-year study window."*

## ERA CLASSIFICATION


Applied to every episode based on `start_year` + `start_month`:


| Era | Date Range | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-COVID | Before March 2020 | Pre-pandemic baseline |
| COVID | March 2020 – December 2021 | WHO pandemic declaration through widespread vaccine rollout |
| Post-COVID | January 2022 – present | Labor market reshuffling period |


This is your primary analytical variable. Era comparison is the headline finding.


---

### Era Statistical Power — Known Limitations

COVID and Post-COVID eras have fewer completed episodes than the 100-episode
minimum due to structural constraints, not data quality issues:

  Pre-COVID:    PASS — sufficient completed episodes, tight CI expected
  COVID:        89 completed — 11 below minimum, wide CI expected
  Post-COVID:   68 completed — 32 below minimum, wide CI expected

This is a feature of studying recent periods where many roles are ongoing.
KM estimates for these eras are valid but less precise.

REQUIRED HANDLING IN ANALYSIS:

1. Do NOT suppress or exclude COVID/Post-COVID curves
2. Confidence bands must be shown — do not hide them
3. Call assert_sufficient_completed_episodes() before each era fit —
   it will issue a warning, which is expected and correct behavior
4. Annotate each era curve directly on the chart with its n= completed
   count so readers can assess precision themselves
5. Post-COVID cohort years 2022–2024 in the cohort trend chart must
   show dashed circles (low_confidence=True) per existing spec

REQUIRED CHART ANNOTATION for COVID and Post-COVID curves:
  Add a small annotation adjacent to each underpowered curve:
  "n=[X] completed episodes — interpret with caution"
  Position: end of the curve line, right-aligned
  Font: 10px, hitchBlueGray, italic

REQUIRED DISCLOSURE LANGUAGE — use verbatim in methodology note
and as a chart caption on Figure 2:

  "COVID and Post-COVID era curves reflect fewer completed episodes
   (89 and 68 respectively) than the pre-registered 100-episode minimum,
   a structural consequence of studying periods where many roles remain
   ongoing. Kaplan-Meier estimates are statistically valid but carry
   wider confidence intervals. This uncertainty reflects genuine market
   volatility in recent cohorts rather than a data deficiency."


## TITLE NORMALIZATION RULES


**Include:**
- Contains: `CISO`, `Chief Information Security Officer`, `Chief Security Officer`, `CSO`
- OR contains both `security` AND one of `VP`, `SVP`, `EVP`, `Vice President` — only if no CISO title exists above them at that company


**Exclude from main analysis (track separately):**
- `Deputy CISO`, `Assistant CISO` → `Excluded_Deputy`
- `Interim CISO`, `Acting CISO` → `Excluded_Interim`
- Pure IT roles (`CIO`, `CTO`, `CITO`) unless security responsibility is explicit → `Excluded_Other`


**`title_normalized` values:** `CISO`, `CSO`, `VP_Security`, `Excluded_Deputy`, `Excluded_Interim`, `Excluded_Other`


Store raw title in `title_raw` always.

## DATE HANDLING RULES


- LinkedIn frequently provides year only, no month
- When month is missing: impute `month = 6` (June), set `imputed_start_month = True` or `imputed_end_month = True`
- When both start and end year are same and no months available: `duration = 6 months`, set `imputed_duration = True`
- Never impute a duration longer than what the data supports
- `scrape_date` is recorded per individual profile, used as censoring date for current roles


**Duration calculation:**
```python
duration_months = (end_year * 12 + end_month) - (start_year * 12 + start_month)
```

## ANALYSES TO PRODUCE


### Analysis 1 — Overall KM Curve (headline figure)
- KM with clustered SE by `profile_id`
- Report median tenure in months + 95% CI
- Plot with confidence bands, median line annotated
- Output: `output/figures/km_overall.png` (300 DPI)


### Analysis 2 — Era Comparison (core finding)
- Separate KM curves for Pre-COVID / COVID / Post-COVID on one plot
- Log-rank test for statistical significance; report p-value on chart
- Three curves in brand colors with confidence bands
- Output: `output/figures/km_by_era.png`


### Analysis 3 — Hazard Rate
- Nelson-Aalen fitter, smoothed with 3-month rolling average
- Identify and annotate peak hazard month
- Save: output/figures/hazard_rate.png


### Hazard Curve Interpretation — Annotate Based on Actual Shape


After fitting the Nelson-Aalen estimator and computing the smoothed hazard,
identify which of the following shapes the curve takes and annotate accordingly.
The shape is a finding in itself and must be labeled on the chart.


SHAPE CLASSIFICATION:


Compute these from the smoothed hazard array:
  - peak_month: month where smoothed hazard is highest
  - peak_value: smoothed hazard value at peak_month
  - early_mean: mean smoothed hazard for months 0–11
  - late_mean: mean smoothed hazard for months peak_month+12 through end
  - first_value: smoothed hazard at month 1
  - last_value: smoothed hazard at final month with n >= 10 in risk set


CLASSIFICATION RULES (evaluate in order, first match wins):


1. HUMP: peak_month is between month 6 and month 60, AND
         peak_value > (early_mean * 1.3), AND
         late_mean < (peak_value * 0.7)
   → Annotation: "Exit risk peaks at month {peak_month}, then declines.
     CISOs who survive past month {peak_month + 12} show significantly
     lower departure rates."


2. DECLINING: first_value > peak_value * 0.85 AND
              last_value < first_value * 0.6
   → Annotation: "Exit risk is highest in the earliest months and
     declines steadily. Role fit is determined quickly — CISOs who
     stay past month {peak_month} tend to remain long-term."


3. RISING: last_value > first_value * 1.4
   → Annotation: "Exit risk increases with tenure. Longer-serving
     CISOs become progressively more likely to depart — consistent
     with role scope expansion and burnout accumulation over time."


4. FLAT: (max smoothed hazard - min smoothed hazard) < (mean smoothed hazard * 0.4)
   → Annotation: "Exit risk is broadly consistent across all tenure
     lengths. No single danger zone dominates — CISO departures are
     distributed across the full tenure spectrum."


CHART ANNOTATIONS TO APPLY:


Always annotate regardless of shape:
  - Vertical dashed line at peak_month in hitchMedTeal (#3B8A7F)
  - Filled circle at peak point
  - Text label: "Peak: Month {peak_month}"
  - Area fill under the smoothed curve in hitchDarkTeal at 6% opacity
    (cumulative hazard accumulation — visually shows where risk concentrates)


Add shape-specific annotation:
  - Text box in upper-right of chart area containing the shape annotation
    sentence from above
  - Font size 11px, hitchBlueGray color, max-width 200px, italic


LOW RISK THRESHOLD:
  After the peak, find the first month where smoothed hazard drops below
  50% of peak_value and stays below for at least 3 consecutive months.
  Call this low_risk_month.
  Add a second vertical dashed line at low_risk_month in hitchLightGray.
  Label: "Risk subsides: Month {low_risk_month}"


  If no such month exists (hazard never drops to 50% of peak):
  Do not draw the second line. Note in chart caption:
  "Hazard does not fall below 50% of peak within the study window."


EXPORT FOR WEB REPORT:
  In addition to the PNG, export output/tables/hazard_data.csv with columns:
    time_months | hazard_rate | hazard_smoothed | is_peak | is_low_risk_threshold
  
  is_peak = True for the single row where hazard_smoothed is maximum
  is_low_risk_threshold = True for the single row identified as low_risk_month
  
  These boolean flags are read by the Next.js HazardChart component to
  position annotations without hardcoding month values.


CAPTION (write dynamically, substituting actual values):
  "Smoothed exit hazard rate across {n_completed} completed CISO episodes.
   Nelson-Aalen estimator with 3-month rolling average. Peak hazard at
   month {peak_month} ({peak_value:.1%} monthly exit rate among survivors).
   [{Shape classification sentence from above}]"




### Analysis 4 — Cohort Trend (the 8-year time series)
- Group COMPLETED episodes only by `start_year` (2017–2024)
- Plot as dot-and-whisker chart (median + 95% CI per year) — NOT a line chart
- Reason: post-2022 cohorts have few completed episodes; a line implies continuity that doesn't exist in thin data
- Add shaded region for COVID era
- Annotate each point with n= completed episodes
- If a year has fewer than 30 completed episodes, display point with dashed border and footnote it as low-confidence
- Output: `output/figures/cohort_trend.png`

- Output: `output/tables/sample_composition_summary.csv`
